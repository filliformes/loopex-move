16-track stereo tape looper · Ableton Move

Loopex

A looping instrument for ambient and experimental music. Sixteen free-running tape loops, four playheads each, a deep set of performance effects, and every gesture click-free.

16 stereo loops 4 playheads / loop 16 punch-in FX 29 send effects 4 reverbs 64 session slots 13 tape models 13 character voicings Schwung Overtake

Built for the Schwung framework on Ableton Move. Free & open source: source on GitHub.

The idea

A looper you play like an instrument

Loopex turns Move's pads into sixteen independent tape loops that never have to agree on a length or a downbeat. You layer sound freely, then treat every loop as raw material: re-pitched, re-ordered, filtered, granulated, frozen, with the whole surface live under your hands.

Free-running

Each loop is its own tape. Record one at 3 seconds and another at 40, in different keys, at different speeds. Nothing is quantized to a grid unless you ask.

Everything performs

Speed, pitch, filter, grain, reverb sends, playheads: all live per loop. Sixteen momentary punch-in effects sit on the right pads, pressure-sensitive and stackable.

Click-free

Every jump, mute, overdub and knob move is crossfaded or smoothed. You can be violent with it and it stays musical.

Loopex draws its screen and widgets with Schwung's own param-page renderer, so it looks and behaves like a native Move instrument: round knobs, notched enum squares, animated buttons, header and footer.
Quickstart

Your first loop in a minute

  1. Open Loopex from the Overtake / Tools list in Schwung. The left 4×4 pads are your loops; the right 4×4 are punch-in effects.
  2. Record. Tap an empty left pad to start recording, tap again to close the loop and play. It loops instantly.
  3. Layer. Tap other empty pads and record more loops on top. They run independently: no need to match lengths.
  4. Shape it. Press a step button to select a loop and see its waveform. Turn the eight knobs to sweep Filter, Pitch, Scatter, Seed and more across its five pages (Up/Down change page).
  5. Punch an effect. Hold a right pad: Haze, Freeze, Chop: over the whole mix. Press harder for more. Shift+pad latches it hands-free.
  6. Send to a reverb. On loop page 1, turn Send A up; open Send FX (track button 3) and choose Veil or Space. Then Save it all to a session with the record button.

Full-exit is Shift+Back. A plain Back only suspends, keeping your loops in memory.

The screen

What you see

Three views. A touched knob takes over the header with the parameter's full name and value; the views fall back to the overview after ten seconds.

Input

What goes onto the tape

Everything you record passes through the input stage first, so set it before you play — it is baked into the take. Track button 1 opens Input, two pages. Input Tape is the machine: monitor level, one of 13 models (Tapeless · Clean · Cass1 · Cass2 · VHS1 · VHS2 · Reel15 · Reel7 · Reel3 · 4trk · Porta · Dub · Warp), trim, drive, wow, flutter, hiss and Generations, the sound of repeated dubbing. Each model’s high-frequency loss is derived from its head gap and tape speed, with a speed-scaled head bump, so the three reel speeds genuinely differ; VHS1 is the Hi-Fi track (an FM carrier with its companding noise reduction, bright but pumping), VHS2 the linear edge track; Generations re-applies the machine’s own loss filter once per pass. Tapeless bypasses the machine but every knob still works, and Drive pushes up to +24 dB into the curve on every model. Input EQ (page 2) blends the Studer 962 presence band with the Tascam 424 MkII/MkIII channel EQ: a 20–800 Hz low cut, the 2–20 kHz tape rolloff, ±15 dB shelves with a sweepable low corner (40–400 Hz, default 100 — the 424’s point) and a 3–15 kHz high corner (default 10 kHz), and a ±12 dB peak sweepable 150 Hz–7 kHz. Every sweep is log. By default the source is the line/mic input; InSrc on Settings page 1 can switch it to the Master mix, any single Schwung or Move track, or Loopex’s own output — see the record-source section below.

Dynamic

The input plays the sampler

On the Capture button. Set Dyn to a mode — or hold Capture to toggle it; the button blinks while it listens — and every phrase you play is captured into a pad by itself. A capture is an ordinary recording: it gets the tape stage, the loop pages, the sends, and its pad goes red then green like any take. What was on the pad is replaced, never stacked (Chase Bliss Onward); Spread is how many pads the sampler rotates through from the selected one — 1 replaces the same pad every time, 16 is a rolling memory of the last sixteen phrases (AC Noises Continua’s dimension, with sixteen layers). Muted pads are skipped, and a pad that already holds a loop is never overwritten until you say so: when the range is full it asks CONTINUE DYNAMIC LOOPING AND OVERWRITE PADS? over whatever view is showing — K6 or K8 yes; K5, Back or opening another menu turns Dynamic off.

Loops & playheads

Sixteen tapes, four heads each

Each loop records up to 45 seconds of stereo audio and plays back on its own clock. Three overdub modes shape how layers build: Replace, Multiply (the default; endless decayed layering), and Disintegration (the loop's own effects re-bake each pass, so it slowly falls apart).

Every loop is read by up to four playheads at once, each with a mode: Off · Fwd · Bwd · Ping · Jump: and its own speed from 0.25× to 4×. Jump leaps to a random position at random intervals, crossfaded. One recording becomes a canon, a drone, or a shifting cloud. Heads sum with equal-power normalisation so stacking never clips, and any head that jumps crossfades.

The five loop pages

Select a loop with a step, then Up/Down to move between pages. Speed re-pitches like tape (pitch + tempo together); Pitch shifts independently, leaving tempo alone. Pitch is a Signalsmith Stretch phase vocoder running on its own thread, off the audio engine, its latency cancelled by nudging the playheads so the loop stays in time. Playback reads are 4-point Hermite with rate-aware anti-imaging; the sampler Characters swap that for linear or drop-sample reads.

HeadMix gives each of the four playheads its own volume and pan (a balance — centre is unity). H1's Vol/Pan are the loop's main level and position (the same controls as page 1); heads 2–4 are independent, defaulting to unity/centre so a loop is unchanged until you touch them.

Seed

A seeded re-order of the loop's slices (2/4/8/16, some reversed). The knob is the seed: every position is a different, repeatable mangle. Click-free.

Scatter

Stochastic slice jumps with crossfades: from gentle stutter to full shuffle.

Tape Wear

The loop wears out like Basinski’s Disintegration Loops: every pass of a playhead flakes a little oxide off that spot, treble first, then level, until it drops out — the damage grows where it started and speeds up at the end. Each track has its own damage, so a hole often opens on one side first and the image wanders as the loop dies. The knob is the rate; 0 freezes the damage, Shift + Undo + pad (or Sessions → Reset) gives you clean tape again. The recording itself is never touched.

Filter + Reso

A DJ-style filter: low-pass left of centre, high-pass right, with resonance. Smoothed, so sweeps never step.

Studer EQ

The 961/962 channel EQ, a Fächerentzerrer (fan equaliser): every setting crosses 0 dB at a ~1 kHz pivot and slopes continuously outward, with no shelf plateau in the band. Bass reaches ±15 dB at 20 Hz, Treble ±15 dB at 20 kHz. Corners and slopes are fitted to the curves printed in Studer’s service manual.

Presence

The console’s Präsenz/Absenz band: a ±11 dB peak at Q = 1, sweepable 150 Hz–7 kHz, to the published spec. Tilt sits alongside it and tips the whole spectrum about 800 Hz.

Sends

Two buses, the Palette engine

Each loop has two sends (A on loop page 1, B on page 2) into a pair of shared effect buses: the Palette engine, 29 effects, chosen in the Send FX menu (track button 3). Every effect has the same three knobs, Amount, Macro and Drift, so a bus can be re-voiced live without relearning it.

The buses are 100% wet: the loop's own level stays dry, the send sets how much reaches the effect. The returns join the master before the punch effects, so a punch can grab a reverb tail too. Every effect is loudness-matched to the dry it replaces, and switching effects morphs — the outgoing one fades as the incoming one fades in — so sweeping through the list never clicks.

Punch-in FX

The right pads: sixteen momentary effects

Hold a pad to apply an effect to the whole mix, reading a rolling 2-second capture of the master. Up to five stack in series, each with four parameters on knobs 5–8, and pad pressure drives a per-effect expression shown in the footer. Oct+, Oct−, Shimmer and Chop spread to stereo through the same microshifter as Stretch and Freeze. The grain effects draw on a 16-grain pool with voice stealing and window-power normalisation.

Loops

Loop12 · Loop16 · LoopSh · Chop. Beat-locked stutters. Chop repeats the last transient on one of 32 rhythmic patterns drawn from Signal. Pressure subdivides.

Grains

Haze · Mosaic · Smear · Strum. Granular clouds: from a shimmering haze to a grid re-sequencer to an arpeggiated cascade. Pressure adds density.

Pitch

Oct− · Oct+ · Glide · Shimmer. Delay-line pitch shifts and a band-limited feedback shimmer that climbs an octave per pass. Pressure pushes regen/glide.

Time

Stretch · Freeze · Reverse · PalFX. Overlap-add stretch and freeze (with a subtle doubler for width), reversed slices, and one whole Palette effect as a punch.

A per-effect LFO on every pad

While a pad is held, knobs 1–4 are its own LFO: Dest (which of the four fx params to modulate), Shape, Rate (0.05–20 Hz) and Depth. Eight shapes: sine, triangle, saw, ramp, square, pulse, sample-and-hold, and smoothed random: for anything from a slow drift to chaotic wobble. Knobs 5–8 stay the effect's parameters.

Shift+pad latches an effect hands-free. Shift while already holding latches it exactly as it is, pressure frozen. Undo+pad resets that pad to its defaults (the LED flashes to confirm).
FX sequencer

The ✕ button: sequencing punch effects

One shared 16-step pattern of punch effects, in the spirit of the Polyend MESS. Each step can hold up to five right pads with their knobs and pressure locked at the moment you write it, so a step keeps sounding the way it did even if you edit the pad later. Perform & master

The master is a performance surface too

The Perform menu (track button 2) has two pages. Page 1 is the Stumble stochastic glitcher plus Jump and Scan (an exact octave up while held). Page 2 is a master processing block:

Master filter

A low-pass with twelve analog characters ported from Fizzik: Clean, SEM, MS-20, Steiner, four ladders, Prophet, Oberheim, Diode/303, K35, Vintage. Cutoff and resonance are smoothed, so sweeps never step. Self-oscillates on the ladder voicings.

Master Clock

A Chase Bliss Mood-style pitch/rate warp with sample-rate crush. Music mode snaps to wide intervals (octaves, fifths, thirds); Free slides continuously. Place it pre- or post-punch.

Ducking pump

K7–K8: a tempo-synced sidechain pump. It ducks on every beat division and breathes back up: from a gentle swell to a hard rhythmic gate.

Tape transport

The ◀ ▶ arrows brake the whole master to a stop or wind it up to a tone, then ease back to speed on release. Fully organic.

Output (Settings page 2)

The master stage, in signal order after the punch bank and Drift: Out · LoCut · HiCut · PWide · Char · gSat · Glue · Limit. Char is thirteen hardware voicings ordered by grit, and it sets far more than an EQ curve: each one chooses the converter (bit depth, sample-rate hold, µ-law companding on the MPC), the saturator’s curve and asymmetry (first-order anti-aliased, so the grit is the machine’s rather than aliasing), the pole count of LoCut/HiCut, how the loops are read (Hermite → linear → drop-sample), and gives Glue and Limit the attack, release, knee and distortion of the dynamics unit that machine shipped beside — the 2254 behind a Neve, the 2500 with its THRUST sidechain behind an API, the SSL bus compressor.

Loop Filter and record source (Settings page 1)

Loop Filter gives every loop the same twelve analog voicings as the Master Filter (Clean, SEM, MS-20, Steiner, four ladders, Prophet, Oberheim, Diode, K35, Vintage): the loop Filter knob sweeps and its low-pass takes on the chosen model, resonance from Reso. Input source (InSrc) chooses what each new recording samples: Line (the line/mic input, default), Master (the whole Move mix, minus Loopex’s own output so it never feeds back), S1–S4 (Schwung’s four mixer slots) M1–M4 (the four OG Move hardware tracks) or Self. Self is Loopex’s own master output, one block old — every loop, the punch chain, both sends and the master stage — so you can resample the whole mix back into a new loop. Unlike Master it has no feedback guard, because the regeneration is the point; the tape limiter at the end of the master chain keeps it saturating rather than running away, and Input Gain is the control. The per-track sources arrive over Ableton Link Audio — the host publishes its slots on /schwung-pub-audio and reconstructs the Move tracks on /schwung-link-in, each as an isolated stereo stem — so a loop can sample any single Schwung or Move track on its own, not just the summed mix (needs host 1.4 with link_audio_publish, on by default). If a stem is not available, Loopex records Line instead and says so on screen while Settings is open.

Settings page 1 also holds arm threshold, overdub mode, root note, input channels (Stereo · Left · Right · Sum — a mono synth on one jack records to both sides with Left), and MIDI In / MIDI Out; page 2 adds the punch-FX stereo width (mono → wide, per effect).

External MIDI

Play it from a LaunchControl XL

A Novation LaunchControl XL drives all sixteen loops from hardware. MIDI In (Settings page 1) enables external control; MIDI Out (Settings page 1) mirrors each loop’s transport back to the LCXL’s LEDs, sent on MIDI channel 2 so its buttons never collide with the Move’s own channel-1 pads.

Buttons

Track Focus (row 1) records, plays and pauses each loop; Track Control (row 2) mutes and unmutes it. The LEDs follow: red recording, green playing, amber paused, red on the control button when muted.

Knobs & faders

Per column the top knob is Loop Speed, then Filter and Pan, and the fader is Volume — for that loop.

Two SysEx templates ship at the repo root — BlackBox 1-8.syx (loops 1–8) and BlackBox 9-16.syx (loops 9–16). Load them onto the LCXL and set both to MIDI channel 2; Loopex accepts either range at once.

Drift

A memory that never quite repeats

On the Sample button: a global drifting-delay memory station in the spirit of Soma COSMOS. Four delay lines of coprime length, each read at a slowly drifting tap, feed back through a matrix that morphs from self-feedback to a normalised Hadamard cross-mix. The loop mix feeds it, the memory recirculates, and because the lengths are coprime and every line has its own asynchronous LFO, the recombination never lands on an exact repeat. It sits in the master chain just before the pump, so its ambient layer takes the character EQ, glue and limiter. Feedback is capped below unity, so the tail always fades: up to a few minutes at the top of the range. Leave everything paused and a silence bleed clears an abandoned tail after about eight seconds with no input. Drift and Mix start at zero, so it stays silent until you dial it in, and it saves with the session.

Drift · Rate · Size

Drift is how much of the loop mix is written into the memory. Rate is how fast the read taps drift. Size sets the tap length, from a close shimmer to a long hall.

FBk · Supr

FBk is the memory sustain: it is capped below unity so the tail always fades, up to a few minutes at maximum. Supr lets loud new input erase the old content, so you replace a layer by playing over it.

Blur · Damp

Blur opens the feedback from each line feeding itself to a full cross-mix between all four, smearing the taps together. Damp rolls the high end off the tail.

Mix

The wet level of the whole Drift layer blended back into the master. A parallel ambient wash under the loops, not a replacement for them.

ASYNC / SYNC

Free, or locked to the bar

Loopex is an asynchronous looper by default: loops are exactly as long as you play them. Set Mode (Sessions page, K8) to SYNC and it locks to Move’s bars and beats — 4/4 bars, triplet ratios included. Switching is always silent: nothing that is playing jumps, and values are only put on the grid the next time you turn them.

Sessions

Save the whole rig

Sixty-four slots on the ≡ (Menu) button. A session stores every setting, the punch-pad values, the FX-sequencer pattern and all recorded audio. Del (K4) erases a slot; Clear (K5) wipes all sixteen loops and resets their settings, leaving the global pages alone; Reset (K6) returns every setting of the selected loop to factory, leaving its audio playing; Reset All (K7) does it for all sixteen. Saving over a used slot, deleting one, clearing or resetting asks first; each is date-and-time named, and the header shows which session is loaded. All disk work happens on a background thread, never on the audio callback, so saving never glitches playback; a second background thread runs the per-loop pitch shifters. Sessions live under /data/UserData/UserLibrary/Loopex/ and survive reinstalls. The audio is stored as plain 16-bit stereo WAV files, one per loop (sessionNN/sNN_loop01.wav …), so you can copy them off the Move and use them anywhere.
